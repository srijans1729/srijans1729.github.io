Working Paper

Alleviating Resource Constraints in the Rural Education Sector of India: Local LLM-based Answer Generation and Evaluation

Kumar, Harsh; Mojumder, Probal; Sen, Srijan

Poor teacher-to-student ratio in rural India is a grave concern. This affects the underprivileged rural students as they can not procure enough study materials. We propose a solution to this by providing answers generated by LLMs catering to their exact needs. This study aims to investigate the optimal condition of LLMs and the associated cost for implementing the system.

Working Paper

AI Search Leads to “Kakistocracy” – How Search-as-Thinking is Reshaped by GenAI

Sen, Srijan; Bhattacharyya, Samadrita

Generative AI (GenAI) is rapidly transforming digital workflows. Users are abandoning traditional search engines. Instead, they favor AI interfaces that deliver instant, pre-synthesized answers. This shift maximizes efficiency. However, it fundamentally alters user behavior and cognitive engagement. Our research introduces the concept of “search-as-thinking”. We ground this in the Effort-Accuracy Framework, Cognitive Fit Theory, and Schema Theory. Conventional search relies on productive cognitive friction. Users must actively formulate queries, evaluate sources, and synthesize information. This friction forces users to build mental schemas. It ultimately drives creative, diversified problem-solving. GenAI search removes this friction. It minimizes mental effort and encourages passive cognitive offloading. This creates an “illusion of mastery” that threatens independent reasoning. Our paper highlights the negative consequences of this digital engagement.

Working Paper

“What Do You See When You Land?” – Context Transfer from Email Marketing to the Online Store

Sen, Srijan; Mojumder, Probal; Mukhopadhyay, Sabyasachi

This study investigates how post-click landing page specificity impacts consumer progression through the e-commerce sales funnel following email marketing campaigns. By analyzing browsing data from an electronics retailer's single-product and multi-product campaigns, the research evaluates how context transfer—aligning the landing page's products with the email creative—affects purchase behavior. The findings reveal that context transfer is a highly consistent, robust driver of conversions. While routing consumers to generic product pages improves outcomes primarily in single-product campaigns, preserving exact product specificity from the email to the destination page significantly amplifies add-to-cart, checkout, and purchase rates across all campaign types. Ultimately, marketers must treat email campaigns as connected journeys, ensuring landing pages actively sustain the specific product cues introduced pre-click.
